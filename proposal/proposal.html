Our final project's name for is Fly Sense. It will be created by Tyler Dinh (dinh.t@wustl.edu, 525483) and Tengis Kelley (k.tengis@wustl.edu, 528529).

Background and Motivation

Recently, a complete map of the male fruit fly's brain and central nervous system was created by Google Research and released in September 2026. The release of the map made it the largest resource for understanding how a brain works. It also pairs nicely with the map of the female fruit fly's brain to understand differences between sexes. According to https://research.google/blog/a-connectomics-milestone-mapping-the-complete-male-fruit-fly-brain/, Google Research uses AI to "turn flat electron microscope images into 3D reconstructions", which has rapidly sped up the release and eventual 100% accuracy of the mapping. Then, AI sets the stage for other animals' brains to be more quickly analyzed like a fish, up until the point where a human's brain could possibly analyzed. If a human's brain is fully mapped and analyzed, it could solve a lot of long-standing research problems and likely revolutionize medicine along with a number of other fields.

Project Objectives

Our primary question that we plan to answer with our visualizations is "How do the output neurons activate for a fruit fly related to one of the five senses when given input stimuli?" Then, we can use the output of the fly's neural network to understand how it may act in any given situation. With the information on how a fly will act, we can better solve the problem of pesky flies, and determine ways to stop them in our lives. For example, there may be a new trap type that homeowners could deploy, based on smell, and features such as sweetness and bitterness. If it is possible to vary these predictor variables, we may be able to determine the best trap type to lure flies and remove them from inside houses. We want to understand this peskiness to better leverage this (for traps, medical research, etc).

Our solution to this primary question is the creation of an interactive visualization to map how different neural pathways in the fly's brain respond to various stimuli and an observation of the output that the fly brain makes.

The benefit of this Researchers can better understand how fly brains work and draw parallels to human brains, ideally. We'll also better understand how to manipulate fly behavior for a desired effect (keeping them out of the house)

Data

We will be collecting our data from `https://male-cns.janelia.org/`. We will download three files that are relevant to our processing and visualization. `data/body-annotations-male-cns-v1.0-minconf-0.5.feather`, `data/body-neurotransmitters-male-cns-v1.0.feather`, and `data/connectome-weights-male-cns-v1.0-minconf-0.5.feather`

Data Processing

Yes, we expect to need substantial data cleanup for Fly Sense. Because displaying the entire neural network of the fly brain is infeasible in the browser through JavaScript, we will need to prune the neural network. We will do this speicifically by only targeting the nodes that are relevant to the selected sense for the fruit fly. For example, for the fruit fly's sense of "taste", we would only target input nodes relevant to tasting, hidden layer nodes relevant to tasting, and output layer nodes relevant to tasting in the neural network.

As such, this will require knowledge of what nodes to prune per each of the five senses we intend to study for a fruit fly. Data processing will be implemented in Python, as it is a much more supported language for processing and analyzing data than JavaScript.

Then, to visualize this neural network, we will use JavaScript for handling our pruned dataset and then rendering & serving it in the browser.
